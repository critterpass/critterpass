Beyond sightseeing, Hoi An is a place to truly experience local culture. You can get tailor-made clothing from skilled tailors, take part in a lantern-making workshop, or hop on a bicycle to explore quiet countryside paths surrounded by rice paddies and coconut groves. And of course, the food here is legendary – dishes like Cao Lau noodles, white rose dumplings, and Hoi An chicken rice are unique to this region and best enjoyed in the bustling night market or a riverside eatery. If you’re looking for a classic day trip from Da Nang, Hoi An Ancient Town is a must Local Tips Best time to go: Arrive in the late afternoon to avoid the midday heat, then stay after dark to see lanterns glowing across the river. How to get there: About 45 minutes from Da Nang. Taxi/Grab costs $15–20 USD one way, but a guided tour saves you the hassle of transport and parking. Lantern Festival: On the 14th day of the lunar month, Hoi An turns off its electric lights and the streets glow only with lanterns – a once-in-a-lifetime experience. Cycling option: Rent a bike ($2–3 USD/day) and ride through the nearby Tra Que Vegetable Village or the coconut palm forest. Tailor shopping: Suits, dresses, or shoes can be made in 24–48 hours. Bring a favorite piece of clothing as a sample for best results. Street food tips: Try banh mi at Madam Khanh or Phuong’s – both famous with locals. Don’t forget to sample Hoi An chicken rice at a family-run eatery. Ticket requirement: Entry to the old town costs about $6 USD, which includes access to several historic houses and assembly halls. Photography tip: Early morning is the quietest time for photos with empty streets, while evening captures the vibrant lantern glow. Cash is king: Many small shops and food stalls don’t accept cards, so bring some Vietnamese dong. Stay overnight option: If your schedule allows, spend a night in Hoi An. The atmosphere is calmer after day-trippers leave, and you’ll get to see the town at its most authentic. Marble Mountains – Caves, Pagodas & Panoramic Views For a mix of nature, culture, and adventure, the Marble Mountains are one of the most rewarding day trips from Da Nang. Just 20 minutes from the city center, this cluster of five limestone and marble hills is named after the five elements – metal, wood, water, fire, and earth – and is filled with caves, Buddhist sanctuaries, and scenic viewpoints. Climbing the stone steps or taking the elevator leads you to a maze of caves, ancient pagodas, and shrines where incense fills the air. Light beams streaming through cave ceilings create an almost mystical feeling. Climbing the mountains rewards you with sweeping views of Da Nang city, My Khe Beach, and even the Cham Islands on a clear day. Inside, the caves create a mystical atmosphere where sunlight streams through openings and incense wafts from the pagodas. For a mix of nature, culture & adventure, Marble Mountains are one of the most rewarding day trips from Da Nang Vietnam Local Tips Best time to visit: Early morning (before 9:30 AM) or late afternoon for cooler weather and fewer crowds. Entrance fee: Around $2 USD per person; the elevator ride costs about $1 USD extra. Dress code: Respectful clothing is required when entering pagodas (cover shoulders and knees). Wear comfortable shoes for the climb. Highlights not to miss: Huyen Khong Cave – Famous for its magical sunlight streaming through the ceiling. Am Phu Cave – A dramatic cave with depictions of heaven and hell, both eerie and fascinating. Xa Loi Tower & Linh Ung Pagoda – Ideal spots for photography and learning about Buddhism in Central Vietnam. Stay prepared: Bring water, a hat, and sunscreen – shade is limited. Souvenirs: Workshops at the base sell marble carvings. While many are large, you can find small jewelry or statues to take home. Transport: About a 15-minute taxi or Grab ride from Da Nang city center ($3–5 USD). Scooter parking costs less than $1 USD. Photography tip: Visit on a sunny day around mid-morning to catch the famous light beams in Huyen Khong Cave. Many people find it tricky to explore all the highlights on their own.
