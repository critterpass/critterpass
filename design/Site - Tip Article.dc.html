How to get six friends to agree on where to go

By Tokek, Bali
SEPT 24, 2026

Most group trips don't fail because people disagree. They fail because nobody is sure when the deciding is supposed to happen, so it never does. Here's how I run it with every crew that lands in Bali.

01Cut it to three

An open question gets forty answers. A list of three gets a vote. Ask everyone for one suggestion, then have whoever is most annoyed by the chat pick the three that fit the budget and the dates. Everything else goes on a "next time" list, which keeps people from feeling ignored.

02Set a deadline you'll keep

"This week" is not a deadline. "Friday at 6pm" is. Put it in the first message and repeat it once, the day before. People who haven't voted by then have chosen to go with the group.

“Agree on the tie-break before anyone votes. After the vote, every rule looks rigged.” — me, every single time

03Pick the tie-break first

Ties happen more than you'd think with six people. Decide now what breaks one: cheapest flights, whoever organised last time gets the call, or a coin flip. Any of them works as long as it was chosen before the result.

04Vote on feel, not logistics

Show each option as a picture and one line about what a day there feels like. Flights and costs matter, but they belong in the shortlist step. If the vote turns into a spreadsheet, the loudest planner wins instead of the best trip.

05Book one thing within a day

A decision without a booking fades by Monday. Lock in one refundable thing, usually the place to stay, within 24 hours of the result. Once something's booked, the trip is real and everyone starts planning for it.
